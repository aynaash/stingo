# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Criticise the code, not
the person who wrote it.

## What that means here

**Good:** "this breaks when the canvas is square, here's a still" · "I think
the beat grid is off by a bar, here's the file" · "I don't understand why
frames have to be pure — can the README say more?"

**Not good:** dismissing someone for not knowing something · derailing a
technical thread into an argument about tooling or licences · personal remarks,
harassment, or sexualised language · publishing someone's private information ·
sustained disruption after being asked to stop.

Not knowing something is the normal state of everyone here. Questions that look
basic are usually documentation bugs.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, and any space where someone is
representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report to **caynaashow@gmail.com**. Reports stay private, including from the
person reported where that is possible.

What happens: I read it, ask you anything I need, and respond — a private word,
an edit or deletion, a temporary block, or a permanent ban, according to how bad
and how repeated it is. I will tell you what I did.

I hold myself to this too. If the problem is me, say so publicly in an issue;
there is no one above me to appeal to, and a public record is the only check
that works.
